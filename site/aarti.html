0:00 – 1:29 AARTI 1

Sukhkarta Dukhharta

॥ सुखकर्ता दुःखहर्ता ॥

The great Ganpati aarti, composed by Samarth Ramdas (17th century). Marathi.

Opening call

मंगलमूर्ती मोरया !

Mangalmurti Morya!

What this means
The call that opens the aarti. Mangalmurti means roughly "the very embodiment of auspiciousness" — one of Ganesh's names. Morya honours Morya Gosavi, a saint who was his great devotee. Calling the two names together is a Maharashtrian habit of affection: the god and his devotee, greeted in one breath.
Verse 1

सुखकर्ता दुःखहर्ता वार्ता विघ्नाची ।
नुरवी पुरवी प्रेम कृपा जयाची ॥
सर्वांगी सुंदर उटी शेंदुराची ।
कंठी झळके माळ मुक्ताफळांची ॥

Sukhkarta Dukhharta Varta Vighnachi
Nurvi Purvi Prem Krupa Jayachi
Sarvangi Sundar Uti Shendurachi
Kanthi Zhalke Mal Muktaphalanchi

What this means
It opens by naming what Ganesh does: he makes happiness and takes sorrow away, and where he is, obstacles leave no trace behind. Then it turns to what you are actually looking at — the idol in front of you, its body bright with vermillion, a string of pearls at the throat. The song moves from idea to image, which is why it works while you're standing before him.
Refrain — everyone

जय देव जय देव जय मंगलमूर्ती ।
दर्शनमात्रे मनकामना पुरती ॥

Jai Dev Jai Dev Jai Mangalmurti
Darshanmatre Mankamana Purti

What this means
The refrain everyone joins. "Victory to you" — and then the line that gives the whole aarti its warmth: you don't have to ask for anything. Simply being in his presence and seeing him is enough for the heart's wish to be met.
Verse 2

रत्नखचित फरा तुज गौरीकुमरा ।
चंदनाची उटी कुंकुमकेशरा ॥
हिरेजडित मुकुट शोभतो बरा ।
रुणझुणती नूपुरे चरणी घागरिया ॥

Ratnakhachit Phara Tuj Gaurikumara
Chandanachi Uti Kumkumkeshara
Hirejadit Mukut Shobhato Bara
Runzhunati Nupure Charani Ghagariya

What this means
A loving description of how the idol has been dressed for the festival — a jewelled backdrop, sandalwood paste and saffron, a crown set with diamonds, anklets that chime when he moves. It's the verse of someone looking closely at the decoration the family has just finished.

→ Refrain repeats

Verse 3

लंबोदर पीतांबर फणिवरवंदना ।
सरळ सोंड वक्रतुंड त्रिनयना ॥
दास रामाचा वाट पाहे सदना ।
संकटी पावावे निर्वाणी रक्षावे सुरवरवंदना ॥

Lambodar Pitambar Phanivarvandana
Saral Sond Vakratunda Trinayana
Das Ramacha Vat Pahe Sadana
Sankati Pavave Nirvani Rakshave Survarvandana

What this means
His forms are named one by one: the generous belly, the yellow silk, the curved trunk, the three eyes. Then the poet steps forward himself — Ramdas, who wrote this in the 1600s, says he's waiting at the door, and asks to be reached in times of crisis and protected at the end.

→ Refrain repeats

In short: He who brings joy and removes sorrow, who leaves no trace of obstacles. His body glows with vermillion, a string of pearls at his throat. Come at the mere sight of you, every wish of the heart is fulfilled.

1:30 – 2:46 AARTI 2

Shendur Lal Chadhayo

॥ शेंदूर लाल चढ़ायो ॥

The Hindi aarti to Ganesh, sung immediately after the Marathi one.

Verse 1

शेंदूर लाल चढ़ायो अच्छा गजमुखको ।
दोंदिल लाल बिराजे सुत गौरीहरको ॥
हाथ लिये गुड़लड्डू साईं सुरवरको ।
महिमा कहे न जाय लागत हूँ पदको ॥

Shendur Lal Chadhayo Achchha Gajmukhko
Dondil Lal Biraje Sut Gaurihar Ko
Hath Liye Gudladdu Sai Survar Ko
Mahima Kahe Na Jay Lagat Hun Pad Ko

What this means
The Hindi aarti begins the same way the Marathi one did — with vermillion on the elephant face — then places him in his family: the son of Gauri and Shankar. He's holding jaggery laddoos. The singer admits his greatness simply can't be described, and so does the only thing left: bows at his feet.
Refrain — everyone

जय जय जी गणराज विद्यासुखदाता ।
धन्य तुम्हारा दर्शन मेरा मन रमता ॥

Jai Jai Ji Ganraj Vidyasukhdata
Dhanya Tumhara Darshan Mera Man Ramta

What this means
Victory to the king of the ganas, who gives both learning and ease. The second line is personal rather than grand — just seeing him, the singer says, settles the mind.
Verse 2

अष्टौ सिद्धि दासी संकटको बैरी ।
विघ्नविनाशन मंगलमूरत अधिकारी ॥
कोटि सूरज प्रकाश ऐसी छबि तेरी ।
गंडस्थल मदमस्तक झूले शशिबिहारी ॥

Ashtau Siddhi Dasi Sankat Ko Bairi
Vighnavinashan Mangalmurat Adhikari
Koti Suraj Prakash Aisi Chhabi Teri
Gandasthal Madmastak Zhule Shashibihari

What this means
The eight siddhis — the classical supernatural powers — are described as attending on him, which is a way of saying nothing is beyond his reach. He is the undoing of trouble itself. His face carries the brightness of countless suns.

→ Refrain repeats

Verse 3

भावभगत से कोई शरणागत आवे ।
संतत संपत सबही भरपूर पावे ॥
ऐसे तुम महाराज मोको अति भावे ।
गोसावीनंदन निशिदिन गुण गावे ॥

Bhavbhagat Se Koi Sharanagat Aave
Santat Sampat Sabhi Bharpur Pave
Aise Tum Maharaj Moko Ati Bhave
Gosavinandan Nishidin Gun Gave

What this means
The promise at the heart of the song: anyone who comes to his shelter with genuine devotion receives abundance that lasts. The final line is the poet signing his own name, as these songs traditionally do — he says he'll sing these praises day and night.

→ Refrain repeats

In short: Vermillion adorns the elephant face; the son of Gauri and Shankar sits holding jaggery laddoos. The eight siddhis serve him, he is the destroyer of obstacles — whoever comes to his shelter with devotion receives abundance.

2:47 – 4:06 AARTI 3

Durge Durghat Bhari

॥ दुर्गे दुर्घट भारी ॥

The aarti to Devi Durga, also by Samarth Ramdas. Marathi.

Verse 1

दुर्गे दुर्घट भारी तुजविण संसारी ।
अनाथनाथे अंबे करुणा विस्तारी ॥
वारी वारी जन्ममरणांते वारी ।
हारी पडलो आता संकट निवारी ॥

Durge Durghat Bhari Tujavin Sansari
Anathnathe Ambe Karuna Vistari
Vari Vari Janmamaranante Vari
Hari Padalo Ata Sankat Nivari

What this means
A change of address — now to the Goddess. Without her, it says, ordinary life is a hard crossing. She is called mother to those who have no one. The last line is unusually honest: the singer says he has simply lost, he is worn out, and asks her to lift the difficulty.
Refrain — everyone

जय देवी जय देवी जय महिषासुरमथिनी ।
सुरवर ईश्वरवरदे तारक संजीवनी ॥

Jai Devi Jai Devi Jai Mahishasurmathini
Survar Ishwarvarade Tarak Sanjivani

What this means
Victory to the one who overcame the buffalo-demon Mahishasura. She gives boons even to the gods, and she is sanjivani — the life-restoring one — who carries people across.
Verse 2

त्रिभुवनभुवनी पाहता तुजऐसी नाही ।
चारी श्रमले परंतु न बोलवे काही ॥
साही विवाद करिता पडिले प्रवाही ।
ते तू भक्तालागी पावसि लवलाही ॥

Tribhuvanbhuvani Pahata Tujaisi Nahi
Chari Shramale Parantu Na Bolave Kahi
Sahi Vivad Karita Padile Pravahi
Te Tu Bhaktalagi Pavasi Lavlahi

What this means
In all three worlds there is no one like her. The four Vedas wore themselves out trying to describe her and still couldn't manage it; the schools of philosophy argued and were swept away in the current. And yet — the turn at the end — she comes quickly to someone who simply loves her. Learning fails where devotion arrives.

→ Refrain repeats

Verse 3

प्रसन्नवदने प्रसन्न होसी निजदासा ।
क्लेशांपासुनि सोडी तोडी भवपाशा ॥
अंबे तुजवाचून कोण पुरविल आशा ।
नरहरि तल्लिन झाला पदपंकजलेशा ॥

Prasannavadane Prasanna Hosi Nijadasa
Kleshanpasuni Sodi Todi Bhavpasha
Ambe Tujavachun Kon Purvil Asha
Narahari Tallin Zhala Padpankajlesha

What this means
She turns a pleased face towards her own; she loosens suffering and cuts the knots that tie us to worldly life. Who else, it asks, would fulfil a hope? The poet Narahari ends absorbed in the dust of her feet.

→ Refrain repeats

In short: O Durga, without you the world is a hard passage. Mother of the motherless, spread your compassion. I am worn out — remove this difficulty. Victory to you, slayer of Mahishasura, giver of boons, the life-giving one who carries us across.

4:07 – 6:13 AARTI 4

Yei Ho Vitthale

॥ येई हो विठ्ठले ॥

The aarti to Vitthal of Pandharpur, signed by Sant Namdev ("Vishnudas Nama"). Marathi.

Refrain — everyone

येई हो विठ्ठले माझे माऊली ये ।
निढळावरी कर ठेवूनि वाट मी पाहे ॥

Yei Ho Vitthale Majhe Mauli Ye
Nidhalavari Kar Thevuni Vat Mi Pahe

What this means
One of the most tender lines in the whole set. Vitthal is addressed not as a lord but as mauli — mother. The image is of someone standing with a hand shading their eyes, watching the road, waiting for a parent to appear.
Verse 1

आलिया गेलिया हाती धाडी निरोप ।
पंढरपुरी आहे माझा मायबाप ॥

Aliya Geliya Hati Dhadi Nirop
Pandharpuri Ahe Majha Maybap

What this means
The waiting continues: the singer sends word with everyone who passes, coming or going, because in Pandharpur — Vitthal's town — is the one who is mother and father to him.

→ Refrain repeats

Verse 2

पिवळा पीतांबर कैसा गगनी झळकला ।
गरुडावरी बैसोनि माझा कैवारी आला ॥

Pivala Pitambar Kaisa Gagani Zhalakala
Garudavari Baisoni Majha Kaivari Ala

What this means
And then, suddenly, arrival. Yellow silk flashes across the sky; his champion has come, riding Garuda the eagle. After two verses of waiting, the god actually shows up.

→ Refrain repeats

Verse 3

विठोबाचे राज्य आम्हा नित्य दिपवाळी ।
विष्णुदास नामा जीवेभावे ओवाळी ॥

Vithobache Rajya Amha Nitya Dipavali
Vishnudas Nama Jivebhave Ovali

What this means
Under Vithoba's reign, it says, every single day is Diwali — the whole year is a festival. The poet signs off as Namdev, servant of Vishnu, waving the lamp with everything he has.

→ Refrain repeats

In short: Come, Vitthal, my mother — I stand with my hand on my brow, watching the road for you. In Pandharpur is my mother and father. My protector has come, riding Garuda. Under Vithoba's reign, every day is Diwali for us.

6:14 – 11:10 AARTI 5

Om Jai Jagdish Hare

॥ ॐ जय जगदीश हरे ॥

The universal aarti (Shraddha Ram Phillauri, 1870). Hindi. All eight verses are sung.

Refrain — everyone

ॐ जय जगदीश हरे, स्वामी जय जगदीश हरे ।
भक्तजनों के संकट, दासजनों के संकट,
क्षण में दूर करे ॥ ॐ जय जगदीश हरे ॥

Om Jai Jagdish Hare, Swami Jai Jagdish Hare
Bhaktajanon Ke Sankat, Dasjanon Ke Sankat,
Kshan Mein Dur Kare — Om Jai Jagdish Hare

What this means
The best-known aarti in India, sung in almost every household. Note that it doesn't name one particular god — it addresses the Lord of the universe, which is why it fits at the end of any puja. Troubles of those who love him are undone in a moment.
Verse 1

जो ध्यावे फल पावे, दुःख बिनसे मन का ।
स्वामी दुःख बिनसे मन का ॥
सुख संपति घर आवे, कष्ट मिटे तन का ॥

Jo Dhyave Phal Pave, Dukh Binse Man Ka
Swami Dukh Binse Man Ka
Sukh Sampati Ghar Aave, Kasht Mite Tan Ka

What this means
Whoever turns their mind to him receives the fruit of it, and the ache in the mind dissolves. Ease and plenty come to the house, and the body's suffering lifts.
Verse 2

मात-पिता तुम मेरे, शरण गहूँ मैं किसकी ।
स्वामी शरण गहूँ मैं किसकी ॥
तुम बिन और न दूजा, आस करूँ मैं जिसकी ॥

Mat-Pita Tum Mere, Sharan Gahun Main Kiski
Swami Sharan Gahun Main Kiski
Tum Bin Aur Na Duja, Aas Karun Main Jiski

What this means
You are my mother and father — so whose shelter would I go to instead? There is no second one. In whom else, it asks, would I even place my hope?
Verse 3

तुम पूरण परमात्मा, तुम अंतरयामी ।
स्वामी तुम अंतरयामी ॥
पारब्रह्म परमेश्वर, तुम सबके स्वामी ॥

Tum Puran Parmatma, Tum Antaryami
Swami Tum Antaryami
Parbrahma Parmeshwar, Tum Sabke Swami

What this means
A more philosophical verse: he is the complete, undivided reality, and at the same time the one who lives inside — antaryami, the indweller who knows your thoughts. Vast and intimate at once.
Verse 4

तुम करुणा के सागर, तुम पालनकर्ता ।
स्वामी तुम पालनकर्ता ॥
मैं मूरख खल कामी, मैं सेवक तुम स्वामी,
कृपा करो भर्ता ॥

Tum Karuna Ke Sagar, Tum Palankarta
Swami Tum Palankarta
Main Murakh Khal Kami, Main Sevak Tum Swami,
Kripa Karo Bharta

What this means
Ocean of compassion, the one who sustains everything. Then a blunt admission: I am foolish, and full of wanting. I am the servant and you are the master — be kind to me.
Verse 5

तुम हो एक अगोचर, सबके प्राणपति ।
स्वामी सबके प्राणपति ॥
किस विध मिलूँ दयामय, तुमको मैं कुमति ॥

Tum Ho Ek Agochar, Sabke Pranpati
Swami Sabke Pranpati
Kis Vidh Milun Dayamay, Tumko Main Kumati

What this means
He can't be reached by the senses, and yet he's the life in everything alive. So the singer asks the honest question — with a mind as small as mine, how would I ever reach you?
Verse 6

दीनबंधु दुःखहर्ता, ठाकुर तुम मेरे ।
स्वामी रक्षक तुम मेरे ॥
अपने हाथ उठाओ, अपने शरण लगाओ,
द्वार पड़ा तेरे ॥

Dinbandhu Dukhharta, Thakur Tum Mere
Swami Rakshak Tum Mere
Apne Hath Uthao, Apne Sharan Lagao,
Dwar Pada Tere

What this means
Friend of the helpless, remover of sorrow. The request becomes physical: put out your hand, pull me into your shelter. I am standing right here at your door.
Verse 7

विषय विकार मिटाओ, पाप हरो देवा ।
स्वामी कष्ट हरो देवा ॥
श्रद्धा भक्ति बढ़ाओ, संतन की सेवा ॥

Vishay Vikar Mitao, Pap Haro Deva
Swami Kasht Haro Deva
Shraddha Bhakti Badhao, Santan Ki Seva

What this means
A request to be changed rather than given things — take away the cravings and the flaws, lift the wrongdoing. Then the positive ask: let my trust and love grow, and let me be of use to good people.
Verse 8

तन मन धन सब कुछ है तेरा ।
स्वामी सब कुछ है तेरा ॥
तेरा तुझको अर्पण, क्या लागे मेरा ॥

Tan Man Dhan Sab Kuchh Hai Tera
Swami Sab Kuchh Hai Tera
Tera Tujhko Arpan, Kya Lage Mera

What this means
The verse that ends most pujas, and perhaps the wisest line in the set. Body, mind, wealth — it is all yours already. I am only handing back what was never mine. So what exactly am I giving you?

→ Refrain repeats, sung several times to close

In short: Victory to the Lord of the universe, who removes the troubles of his devotees in an instant. You are my mother and father; whose shelter else would I seek? All I have is already yours — what is there in it that is mine to give?

11:11 – 12:22 AARTI 6

Ghalin Lotangan

॥ घालीन लोटांगण ॥

The closing prayer — the surrender. Marathi verse by Sant Namdev, followed by three Sanskrit shlokas and the mahamantra.

The surrender (Marathi)

घालीन लोटांगण वंदीन चरण ।
डोळ्यांनी पाहीन रूप तुझे ॥
प्रेमे आलिंगिन आनंदे पूजिन ।
भावे ओवाळीन म्हणे नामा ॥

Ghalin Lotangan Vandin Charan
Dolyanni Pahin Rup Tujhe
Preme Alingin Anande Pujin
Bhave Ovalin Mhane Nama

What this means
The closing prayer, and a complete act of worship in four lines: I'll lie full-length on the ground, bow at your feet, look at your form, embrace you, worship you, and wave the lamp — each step done with love rather than duty. It ends with Namdev naming himself.
Shloka 1 — Tvameva Mata

त्वमेव माता च पिता त्वमेव ।
त्वमेव बंधुश्च सखा त्वमेव ॥
त्वमेव विद्या द्रविणं त्वमेव ।
त्वमेव सर्वं मम देवदेव ॥

Tvameva Mata Cha Pita Tvameva
Tvameva Bandhushcha Sakha Tvameva
Tvameva Vidya Dravinam Tvameva
Tvameva Sarvam Mama Devadeva

What this means
A short Sanskrit verse most people here will know by heart. You alone are my mother and father, my brother and my friend, my learning and my wealth — you are everything to me. It's often the first Sanskrit a child is taught.

You alone are my mother and father, my kin and my friend, my learning and my wealth — you are my everything, God of gods.

Shloka 2 — Kayena Vacha

कायेन वाचा मनसेंद्रियैर्वा ।
बुद्ध्यात्मना वा प्रकृतिस्वभावात् ॥
करोमि यद्यत् सकलं परस्मै ।
नारायणायेति समर्पयामि ॥

Kayena Vacha Manasendriyairva
Buddhyatmana Va Prakriti Svabhavat
Karomi Yadyat Sakalam Parasmai
Narayanayeti Samarpayami

What this means
Whatever I do — with my body, my speech, my mind, my senses, or simply out of habit and nature — I hand all of it over. The point isn't to stop acting, but to stop clutching at the results of what you do.

Whatever I do — with body, speech, mind or senses — I offer it all to Narayana.

Shloka 3 — Achyutam Keshavam

अच्युतं केशवं रामनारायणं ।
कृष्णदामोदरं वासुदेवं हरिं ॥
श्रीधरं माधवं गोपिकावल्लभं ।
जानकीनायकं रामचंद्रं भजे ॥

Achyutam Keshavam Ram-Narayanam
Krishna-Damodaram Vasudevam Harim
Shridharam Madhavam Gopikavallabham
Janakinayakam Ramchandram Bhaje

What this means
A garland of names strung together, each one a different face of Vishnu — Achyuta, Keshava, Rama, Narayana, Krishna, Damodara, Vasudeva, Hari, Shridhara, Madhava, the beloved of the gopis, the husband of Janaki. Singing the names is itself the worship.
Mahamantra

हरे राम हरे राम राम राम हरे हरे ।
हरे कृष्ण हरे कृष्ण कृष्ण कृष्ण हरे हरे ॥

What this means
The great chant. No request and no argument — only the names of Rama and Krishna, repeated. Sung twice through here.

Morya Re Bappa Morya Re

What this means
Call and response, and everyone joins in — it gets faster each time round. Bappa means father, the affectionate name. Again the god and his devotee Morya Gosavi are called together. It feels like an ending, but it isn't quite: it leads into the Mantrapushpanjali.

Ganpati Bappa Morya

12:47 – 14:10 AARTI 8

Mantrapushpanjali

॥ मंत्रपुष्पांजली ॥

The Vedic closing hymn — the "offering of flowers of mantra". Sanskrit, from the Taittiriya Aranyaka. Traditionally everyone stands for this one.

Verse 1

ॐ यज्ञेन यज्ञमयजन्त देवास्तानि धर्माणि प्रथमान्यासन् ।
ते ह नाकं महिमानः सचन्त यत्र पूर्वे साध्याः सन्ति देवाः ॥

Om Yajnena Yajnamayajanta Devastani Dharmani Prathamanyasan
Te Ha Nakam Mahimanah Sachanta Yatra Purve Sadhyah Santi Devah

What this means
The name means "an offering of flowers made of mantra" — the flowers are the words themselves. This first verse is very old, from the Vedas: by offering, the gods honoured the act of offering, and those first rites became the pattern; in that way they reached the heights where the earlier gods dwell.
Verse 2

ॐ राजाधिराजाय प्रसह्य साहिने ।
नमो वयं वैश्रवणाय कुर्महे ॥
स मे कामान् कामकामाय मह्यम् ।
कामेश्वरो वैश्रवणो ददातु ॥
कुबेराय वैश्रवणाय । महाराजाय नमः ॥

Om Rajadhirajaya Prasahya Sahine
Namo Vayam Vaishravanaya Kurmahe
Sa Me Kaman Kamakamaya Mahyam
Kameshwaro Vaishravano Dadatu
Kuberaya Vaishravanaya — Maharajaya Namah

What this means
A salutation to the king of kings, and to Kubera, keeper of the world's wealth — asking that what is desired be granted. In this setting it is less about riches than about being given what is genuinely needed.
Verse 3

ॐ स्वस्ति । साम्राज्यं भौज्यं स्वाराज्यं वैराज्यं पारमेष्ठ्यं राज्यं महाराज्यमाधिपत्यमयम् ।
समन्तपर्यायी स्यात्सार्वभौमः सार्वायुष आन्तादापरार्धात् ।
पृथिव्यै समुद्रपर्यन्ताया एकराळिति ॥

Om Svasti — Samrajyam Bhaujyam Svarajyam Vairajyam Parameshthyam Rajyam Maharajyamadhipatyamayam
Samantaparyayi Syatsarvabhaumah Sarvayusha Antadaparardhat
Prithivyai Samudraparyantaya Ekaraliti

What this means
A prayer for good governance and well-being — for sovereignty, self-rule and freedom extending across the whole earth as far as the sea, and lasting a full lifetime. Notice who it is for: not the singer, but everyone.
Verse 4

तदप्येष श्लोकोऽभिगीतो ।
मरुतः परिवेष्टारो मरुत्तस्यावसन् गृहे ॥
आविक्षितस्य कामप्रेर्विश्वेदेवाः सभासद इति ॥

Tadapyesha Shloko'bhigito
Marutah Pariveshtaro Maruttasyavasan Grihe
Avikshitasya Kamaprervishvedevah Sabhasada Iti

What this means
It closes by recalling King Marutta, in whose household even the storm-gods served and the gods themselves sat in the assembly — a picture of a realm so well ruled that heaven was at home in it. That is the note the whole aarti ends on.

In short: A prayer not for oneself but for the wellbeing of all — for good governance, prosperity and peace across the whole earth to the seas. It is the traditional way to end, offered standing, with folded hands.
